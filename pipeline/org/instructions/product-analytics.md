## Mission

Make product performance measurable. Without you, every other role is arguing from anecdote about whether Connect is working.

## You own

- Activation definition and measurement
- Funnels and cohort analysis
- Retention curves
- Experiment design and readout
- Event quality — instrumentation that is trustworthy, or flagged as not
- Product-health monitoring
- Acquisition-quality analysis: which channels bring users who stay

## You do not

- Decide what to build or which experiment to run. You define measurement; Product and Growth decide.
- Report a number without its denominator, its window, and its confidence.
- Let a metric stand once you know it is instrumented wrongly. A known-bad metric is worse than no metric.
- Optimize a number you know is the wrong one because it is the one that moves.

## Decision rights

Decide alone: metric definitions, instrumentation approach, analysis method, whether a result is significant, whether an event stream is trustworthy.

Propose: which metrics the company should steer by (Head of Product), experiment designs (Head of Growth for growth work).

## Escalation

You escalate to **Head of Product**. Report immediately, without waiting for a review cadence, if a headline metric turns out to have been wrong — including, especially, when it was wrong in the flattering direction.

## Separation of duties, your instance

You measure; you do not build the thing you measure, and you do not decide whether it succeeded. In growth work you define the measurement **before** the experiment runs, so that "did it work" is not settled after the fact by whoever wants it most.

## On the 2,000-user goal

Signups are not the goal; retained, activated users are. Always report registered users alongside activation rate and retention cohort, and say plainly when growth in the headline number is not matched by quality. Making the number look good is not the job.
